# Rootwaker — Animal Guardians & Coronation Redesign

**Date**: 2026-08-17
**Status**: Draft — building autonomously per standing loop directive, no user review gate this cycle
**Supersedes**: replaces `mountainGuard`'s and the King's visual/mechanical identity from the mountain-ascent chapter (`docs/specs/2026-08-16-rootwaker-mountain-ascent-chapter-design.md`) and the throne-room chapter (`docs/specs/2026-08-16-rootwaker-throne-room-chapter-design.md`). Does NOT touch the mountain-ascent's climb-segment terrain mechanic — that is a separate, larger follow-up pass (see "Explicitly out of scope" below).
**Scope**: real-animal identity for every jungle/mountain antagonist, an ability earned from every defeated animal (not just the 2 chapter-2 prey species), guard encounters relocated into the jungle chapter itself, and a coronation ending replacing the current "village welcomes you home" beat.

## Why this redesign, why now

The user gave direct feedback immediately after the throne-room chapter shipped: enemies should read as real animals with real animal powers, not humanoid constructs; every animal the fox defeats should grant a power, matching the pattern already used for the 2 hunted prey species; the mountain's guards belong in the jungle, not stationed on climb ledges; and the ending should be a coronation — the fox becomes the new King of the Mountain — not a village-welcome tableau. This is a real identity reframe of already-shipped systems, not a bug fix, so it gets its own design doc and plan, same discipline as every prior chapter.

## What this redesign changes

- **Mountain guards become a real animal — the Grove Bear.** Currently `mountainGuard.ts` builds a stone/armored humanoid (box torso, icosahedron head, cone shoulder-trim) positioned on the mountain-ascent's climb ledges. Redesigned as a real bear: a heavier, low-slung quadruped-posture body (reusing the procedural `Rig`+`Clip` system exactly like `tuskBoar.ts`'s quadruped rig, scaled up), with a genuine bear attack — a heavy claw swipe (reusing `EnemyAI`'s existing telegraph/attack pattern, no new state machine) — instead of a generic melee strike. Same HP/damage numbers as the current `mountainGuard` (65 HP, 12 damage) so difficulty tuning doesn't need to be re-balanced from scratch.
- **The King becomes a real animal — the Elder Bear King.** Currently `createMountainKing.ts` builds a scaled-up stone/crowned humanoid. Redesigned as a much larger, elder Grove Bear — visually distinct from the regular Grove Bear the way `mountainGuard`/`createMountainKing` were already distinct in scale, but now sharing a real animal silhouette family instead of an unrelated humanoid-vs-animal split. Keeps the already-shipped, already-reviewed 2-phase escalation (calm/enraged via `BossPhaseController`, the ground-slam hazard armed only in the enraged phase — a bear rearing up and slamming down reads naturally as this exact mechanic, no redesign needed there) and the exact HP/damage numbers (220 HP, 12/18 damage by phase).
- **Every defeated animal grants a real ability, not just the 2 prey species.** `AbilityKit`'s existing pattern (`keen-ear` from the grove-hare, `boar-charge` from the tusk-boar) extends to the Grove Bear and the Elder Bear King: defeating a Grove Bear grants `bear-swipe` (a heavier melee option — thematically "the fox learns to hit like a bear"), and defeating the Elder Bear King grants `kings-roar` (a fear/stagger effect on nearby enemies — the "a king's presence commands respect" payoff, tying directly into the coronation ending below). Both new abilities follow `AbilityKit.ts`'s exact existing shape (`AbilityId` union, `ABILITIES` record, `unlock()` call site at the moment of the kill), no new unlock mechanism invented.
- **Grove Bears relocated into the jungle chapter.** Currently 2 `mountainGuard` instances stand on mountain-ascent ledges (ledge 2 and ledge 3). Redesigned Grove Bears are placed in the jungle chapter itself (`createJungleLevel.ts`, alongside the existing tusk-boar/grove-hare wildlife), reachable and fightable before the player ever begins the mountain climb — matching the user's framing: "guards in the jungle if fox battle with them and kill them and then reach the mountain." The mountain-ascent's climb-ledge rest points remain hazard-free stamina checkpoints (as originally designed), no longer needing a stationed guard, since the guard encounter now happens earlier, in the jungle.
- **Coronation ending replaces "the village welcomes you home."** The throne-room chapter's just-shipped victory sequence (open the gate, reveal a village tableau, "The village welcomes you home") is replaced with a coronation beat: on defeating the Elder Bear King, the fox is crowned — a real visual beat (the fox's own model gains a simple crown/regalia addition, reusing `createMountainKing`'s existing crown-spike-cone construction technique scaled to fit the fox), the throne room's gate still opens and the village tableau still reveals (the village accepting its new king, not just "welcoming a visitor"), and the HUD objective text changes to reflect the fox's new title (e.g. "You are the new King of the Mountain.") — same underlying `openGate()`/`villageMeshes`/HUD-toast machinery already built, only the narrative framing and the fox's own visual state change.

## What stays exactly as built

- `Rig`+`Clip` procedural animation system, `EnemyAI`'s telegraph/combat state machine (unmodified — same constraint as every prior chapter), `BossPhaseController`'s calm/enraged HP-threshold escalation, `GroundSlam`'s telegraph-then-effect hazard shape — all reused directly for the redesigned Grove Bear/Elder Bear King, since the MECHANICS the user praised (telegraphed, fair, escalating combat) were never the complaint — only the VISUAL IDENTITY (humanoid constructs) was.
- `AbilityKit`'s existing unlock/report shape — extended with 2 new ability IDs, not redesigned.
- The throne-room arena geometry, summit-gate trigger, and boss HP bar — unchanged; only the victory sequence's narrative content changes.
- The mountain-ascent's climb-segment/ledge/stamina mechanic — explicitly NOT touched by this pass (see below).

## Explicitly out of scope for this pass

- **The mountain-ascent's climb-segment terrain mechanic** (currently a sequence of thin vertical wall segments). The user also asked for "reach the mountain by climbing it not the wall" — a request to make the climb itself read as a real mountain landform rather than an abstracted wall-climbing mechanic. This is a substantial, separate terrain-geometry rework (likely touching `createJungleLevel.ts`'s `buildMountain`/`buildClimbSegment` significantly, and possibly `PlayerController`'s climbing locomotion) with real risk of its own (this project's history shows level-geometry changes are exactly where coordinate bugs hide). Scoped to its own future pass, once this animal-identity pass has shipped and been verified, matching this project's own "one real system change at a time" discipline used for every prior chapter.
- **Any new fox ability beyond `bear-swipe`/`kings-roar`.** Exactly 2 new abilities, matching the existing 2-ability pattern's scale.
- **Any change to jungle chapter's existing wildlife (hares, tusk-boars) or their AI.** The Grove Bear is a new, additional jungle inhabitant, not a replacement for anything already there.
- **Village NPC interactivity.** The coronation ending is still a tableau beat (per the throne-room chapter's own explicit scoping), not a playable village hub.

## Success criteria

A player who fights their way through the jungle should encounter and defeat at least one real Grove Bear before reaching the mountain, earning `bear-swipe` from it — a tangible, animal-themed power-up that reads as "I beat a bear and now hit harder," not a generic loot drop. Reaching the throne room and defeating the Elder Bear King should feel like defeating a bigger, elder version of an animal already fought once, not an unrelated final boss. The ending should read as "the fox is now the mountain's king," not "the fox visited a village" — a real narrative payoff distinct from every prior chapter's ending.
